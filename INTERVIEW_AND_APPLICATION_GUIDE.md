# Interview and Application Guide

## Use the portfolio accurately

Describe these as **simulated portfolio projects**. Explain the artifacts you can personally defend, and verify each statement before using it on a résumé. Modeled results must never be presented as employer accomplishments.

## Role-specific walkthroughs

| Role | Situation and task | Action to discuss | Result and limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project Coordinator | CRM release with dependencies and optional scope | WBS, RAID, change cost and acceptance gates | Demonstrated release judgment; no actual deployment |
| Operations Analyst | Labor cost versus customer service | Compare options, break-even, absence response | Quantified a fictional trade-off; no verified savings |
| Product Operations Analyst | Checklist pilot shows higher activation and more escalations | Cohort eligibility, guardrails and limited rollout | Recommended further testing; no causal claim |
| People Operations Coordinator | Readiness and check-ins have different eligible cohorts | Define denominators and route exceptions | Clearer measurement and accountability in a simulation |
| Team Lead | Missed deadlines and conflicting priorities | Coaching, capacity adjustment and delegation | Worked leadership response; no claim of actual reports |

## Example résumé bullets — portfolio/project section only

- Developed a simulated CRM implementation case with a 10-week delivery plan, responsibility matrix, cost forecast, change-control analysis, and requirement-to-test traceability.
- Analyzed a fictional retail staffing decision using labor cost, service guardrails, break-even assumptions, and a recovery response to staff absences.
- Evaluated a synthetic customer-onboarding pilot using activation rates, escalation guardrails, cohort eligibility, and limitations of a before/after design.
- Created management work samples covering supplier contingency, employee readiness, coaching, delegation, and executive status reporting.

Avoid “saved $X,” “managed a team of X,” or “increased retention by X” unless those statements describe your actual verified experience. These examples intentionally describe the work product.

## Five-minute interview structure

1. **30 seconds:** state the fictional business problem and simulation boundary.
2. **60 seconds:** show one artifact and explain your analysis.
3. **90 seconds:** compare options and defend a trade-off.
4. **60 seconds:** trace a calculation to inputs and assumptions.
5. **60 seconds:** describe uncertainty, a failed guardrail, and what you would do next.

## Prepare to answer

- Why can a 97% test pass rate still be a no-go?
- How is a percentage-point change different from relative improvement?
- Why is marketing revenue return different from profit ROI?
- Why cannot a newly hired cohort have a measured 90-day retention rate in its first week?
- What evidence would change your recommendation?
- How would you validate the workbook after editing an input?
- How do you distinguish completed tasks from realized benefits?

## Live demonstration practice

Open a project source CSV, explain one metric's unit, recompute one endpoint, then show the matching README statement. In Excel, trace a formula and change one input in a disposable copy; explain the downstream effect. In the leadership case, explain the boundary between delegated work and retained accountability.

A portfolio supports a job application. It does not replace role-specific experience requirements or prove proficiency with tools you have not used.
