# Leadership Case — Coaching, Conflict and Delegation

> **Simulated work sample:** All events, people, approvals, records, costs, and outcomes below are fictional portfolio exercises. They are not employer results or evidence of a live system implementation.

## Situation and leadership objective

A fictional implementation team is preparing a release while Operations wants faster delivery and Support is concerned about readiness. One analyst has missed two handoff deadlines. The lead must protect delivery, understand the missed commitments, and maintain a fair working relationship.

**Success:** clear ownership, recoverable commitments, safe release conditions and a workable team capacity plan. Success is not defined as getting everyone to agree with the lead.

## Diagnose before responding

Review the two missed deliverables, original deadlines, scope changes, workload, dependencies and earlier communications. Separate observable behavior (“handoff checklist arrived two days late”) from character judgments (“unreliable”). Invite the analyst's explanation privately.

In this exercise, the analyst carries two urgent assignments and waited for a decision from Sales Ops. The lead accepts responsibility for assigning conflicting priorities and for unclear escalation timing.

## Coaching conversation record

| Element | Worked example |
|---|---|
| Observation | Two readiness checklists missed agreed handoff dates |
| Impact | Support could not finish preparation; release risk increased |
| Employee perspective | Competing urgent requests and unresolved access mapping |
| Agreed change | Lead publishes one priority order; analyst flags blockers within one working day |
| Support provided | Remove one noncritical report and pair with a senior analyst for mapping |
| Follow-up | Ten-minute review twice weekly for two weeks |
| Evidence of progress | Commitments met or renegotiated before deadline, plus checklist quality review |

This is an informal coaching exercise, not a fabricated disciplinary record. A real employment process would follow the employer's policies and involve the appropriate manager/HR partners.

## Delegation with boundaries

| Task | Delegate | Authority granted | Boundary / escalation | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maintain readiness checklist | Analyst | Request updates and flag missing evidence | Cannot mark approvals complete for others | Daily during release week |
| Coordinate training sessions | Coordinator | Arrange sessions within agreed calendar | Cannot waive attendance or proficiency criteria | Twice weekly |
| Triage support questions | Support Lead | Route and prioritize within support policy | Security/data exposure escalates immediately | Daily |
| Approve release | Business/technical decision owners | Acceptance within their accountabilities | Lead cannot substitute for their approval | Formal readiness review |

Delegation includes the expected output, due date, available resources and definition of done. Retaining accountability does not require doing every task personally.

## Conflict resolution

Operations requests Friday release to meet a commercial date. Support requests another week. Both positions have legitimate costs.

1. Establish shared interests: continuity, usable workflow, manageable support demand.
2. Ask each party for evidence: business cost of delay, unresolved readiness gaps, support capacity.
3. Compare options: full release; limited pilot; delayed release.
4. Agree non-negotiable conditions: no unresolved critical access defect, reconciled data, and named support ownership.
5. Escalate the decision to the accountable sponsor if residual trade-offs exceed the lead's authority.

**Simulated decision:** limited pilot only after critical gates pass, followed by review before wider rollout. If a critical gate fails, delay. Commercial pressure does not change what the evidence shows.

## Resource trade-off

Team has 80 available hours for the next week. Mandatory readiness work requires 56; support preparation requires 16; optional reporting requires 20. Demand is 92 hours, exceeding capacity by 12.

Defer all 20 optional reporting hours, allocate 72 committed hours, and keep 8 hours of contingency. Do not assume 12 hours of overtime is free or sustainable. Publish what was deferred and who approved the revised priority.

## After-action review

The fictional two-week follow-up records four handoff commitments: three completed on time and one renegotiated one day before due date because a dependency remained unresolved. The checklist quality review finds one missing support contact, corrected before acceptance.

This is improvement in visibility and ownership, not perfect performance. Next action: make support-contact validation mandatory and review whether workload remains realistic. Ask the team what leadership behavior helped or hindered delivery.

## Interview discussion

Explain what you personally authored in this exercise, what information would be needed in a real setting, which decision was outside your authority, and why coaching addressed both system problems and individual accountability.
