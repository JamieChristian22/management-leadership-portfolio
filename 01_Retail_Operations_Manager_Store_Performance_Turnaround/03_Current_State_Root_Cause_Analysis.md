# Current-State & Root-Cause Analysis

## Problem Statement
The store is not suffering from one isolated KPI miss. The current state reflects a breakdown in operating discipline across planning, execution, visibility and follow-through.

## Root-Cause Structure

### Labor Cost
**Symptoms:** 18.9% labor cost, uneven coverage, overtime/late adjustments.
**Likely causes:** schedules built from static patterns; limited daypart demand alignment; reactive call-out coverage; insufficient actual-vs-plan review.
**Evidence to validate:** traffic by hour, scheduled vs worked hours, overtime, transactions per labor hour.

### Customer Experience
**Symptoms:** 8.4-minute wait, NPS 52.
**Likely causes:** backup coverage triggers unclear; front-end staffing not synchronized to traffic; slow escalation; service recovery inconsistent.
**Evidence:** queue observations, register utilization, complaint themes, NPS comments.

### Shrink
**Symptoms:** 3.8% shrink.
**Likely causes:** inconsistent cycle counts; high-risk SKU exceptions not closed; receiving variance; unclear accountability.
**Evidence:** adjustment history, count variance, receiving exceptions, department concentration.

### Execution
**Symptoms:** 71% task completion, 74% schedule adherence.
**Likely causes:** ownership ambiguity; checklists not treated as controls; follow-up delayed; competing priorities not escalated.
**Evidence:** missed-task log, attendance/timekeeping, checklist completion, aged actions.

## 5-Why Example — Long Wait Time
1. Why are customers waiting? Checkout capacity is insufficient at peaks.
2. Why is capacity insufficient? Backup registers are opened late.
3. Why are they opened late? Trigger thresholds are not standardized.
4. Why are thresholds not standardized? Queue management is dependent on individual judgment.
5. Why is judgment inconsistent? No documented control, owner, evidence or escalation exists.

**Root cause:** queue management is not operating as a defined control.

## Prioritization
Priority 1: service/labor controls because they affect customers daily.
Priority 2: execution discipline because it enables every workstream.
Priority 3: shrink because financial impact compounds and requires sustained controls.
