# People Operations Cohort and Manager Review

> **Simulated work sample:** All events, people, approvals, records, costs, and outcomes below are fictional portfolio exercises. They are not employer results or evidence of a live system implementation.

## Measurement discipline

The original weekly report mixes current new-hire activity with 90-day metrics. Interpret 90-day measures as snapshots of earlier cohorts whose observation windows have matured, not as outcomes for hires who started that same week. The source file does not include cohort IDs or numerator counts, so those rates cannot be reconstructed as person-level outcomes.

This independent exercise supplies explicit denominators.

| Measure | Eligible population | Successful | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Day-1 readiness | 40 current starts | 38 | 95% |
| 30-day manager check-in | 25 employees reaching day 30 | 23 | 92% |
| 90-day retention | 20 earlier hires reaching day 90 | 19 | 95% |
| 90-day role readiness | 19 retained hires eligible for assessment | 17 | 89.5% |

Two different readiness denominators answer different questions. Retained-hire readiness is 17/19; readiness across the original 20-person cohort is 17/20 = 85%. State which one is used; never silently exclude leavers to improve the rate.

## Readiness exception review

| Case | Problem | Immediate action | Owner | Deadline / closure evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N01 | Laptop not delivered | Approved loan device with required controls | IT | Before start; asset assignment confirmed |
| N02 | Required access missing | Role-based access request and approved training alternative | IT / Manager | Day 1; access verified |
| N03 | Day-30 check-in missed | Reschedule and identify workload barrier | Manager | Two working days; conversation recorded as complete |
| N04 | Training complete but task demonstration failed | Targeted practice and reassessment | Team Lead | Five working days; rubric-based demonstration |

Training attendance is not competence. Check-in completion is not conversation quality. Readiness metrics must be accompanied by qualitative review.

## Manager coaching exercise

Two missed check-ins are observed for the same fictional manager. People Ops meets privately, states dates and expected standard, and asks what prevented follow-through. The manager reports coverage shortages and unclear responsibility during leave.

Agreed response: delegate calendar coordination to a team coordinator; manager retains the coaching conversation; appoint an approved backup for leave; review completion and employee feedback after two weeks. If the pattern repeats, escalate through the organization's established performance process rather than inventing penalties.

## Privacy and fair handling

Use fictional IDs in public artifacts. Actual employee data would be restricted by role and purpose, with retention rules defined by the employer. Escalation records should capture operational facts, not unnecessary medical or personal details.

A retention-risk flag triggers a supportive human review, not an automated adverse employment decision. Do not infer motivation from missing training or absence alone. Ask about tools, workload, accessibility, role clarity and support.

## Capacity and investment exercise

Assume 40 hires/month and 1.5 hours of avoidable administrative rework per hire at $30/hour. Gross opportunity = 40 × 1.5 × $30 = **$1,800/month**. Standardization costs 20 hours × $40 = **$800**. At 50% realized rework reduction, benefit is $900/month before ongoing maintenance; simple payback is 0.89 months.

This is a time-value estimate, not cash savings or a justification for reducing staff. Track actual rework and implementation cost before claiming a benefit.

## Sustainment decision

People Ops reviews exceptions weekly; managers own role clarity and follow-up; IT owns technical readiness. At 30 days review denominators and root causes. Scale only if readiness improves without increased manager burden, unresolved access exceptions or adverse employee feedback.
